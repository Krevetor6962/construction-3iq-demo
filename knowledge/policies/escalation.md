# Synthetic escalation and ownership policy

## Escalation-1: Trigger conditions
Escalate an informal promise conflict, a late inbound delivery, missing qualification evidence, or a proposed allocation change that affects another customer. Identify the disagreement rather than treating collaboration text as a replacement for structured supply facts.

## Escalation-2: Fictional owners
All collaborators are synthetic. `supply.planner@example.com` verifies quantities and dates; `sales.manager@example.com` is the approval owner; `qualification.reviewer@example.com` handles documented qualification. Each `account.owner.NNN@example.com` coordinates its fictional account and obtains customer acceptance.

## Escalation-3: Golden promise conflict
For `REC-CON-0003`, the fictional office account owner informally promised all 400 m2. The policy-based allocation proposal is 0 m2. Flag the conflict, route it to the supply planner and sales manager, and prepare a corrected draft-only communication. Do not claim the customer has accepted a change.

## Escalation-4: No autonomous execution
The demo proposes an owner checklist and approval checkpoint. It does not send emails, approve substitutions, modify orders, change allocations, or activate campaigns.
